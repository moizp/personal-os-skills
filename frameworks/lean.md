# Framework: Lean Principles (Lean Startup / Lean Thinking)

Generic summary for use by Skills.

## Core ideas relevant to planning

- **Test the riskiest assumption first.** Before committing to a full
  quarter of work on a goal, identify the assumption that, if wrong,
  invalidates the whole plan — and find the cheapest way to test it before
  investing further. Not every assumption needs testing; only the ones
  the plan can't survive being wrong about.

- **Build-Measure-Learn loop.** Ship the smallest thing that produces a
  real signal, measure the actual outcome (not just completion), and let
  that update the plan — rather than executing a full plan and only
  finding out at the end whether the premise held.

- **MVP / smallest viable test.** The test of an assumption should be the
  cheapest version that still produces a decisive answer — not the full
  final version of the goal.

- **Timeboxing.** Give a task or experiment a fixed, small time budget
  rather than "however long it takes." Timeboxing forces prioritisation
  (what can actually get done in the box) and surfaces stalled work
  quickly instead of letting it silently expand.

- **Small batch sizes / limit work in progress.** Fewer things in flight
  at once means faster feedback per item and less sunk-cost pressure to
  keep going on something that isn't working. This is also the underlying
  logic of a Kanban board's WIP limits, not just its columns.

- **Eliminate waste.** Periodically ask whether a recurring task or
  process step still produces value, or is being done from habit/inertia.

## How this should show up in the system

- Quarterly goal-setting should identify each goal's riskiest assumption
  and propose a cheap, timeboxed test for it before full commitment,
  rather than assuming the goal is valid as stated.
- Daily/weekly planning should timebox items, not just list them —
  especially exploratory or uncertain work.
- Weekly review should ask what was *learned*, not just what was
  completed — a "failed" timeboxed experiment that produced a clear
  answer is a success in Lean terms.
- Works well alongside GTD (both value externalising and clarifying work)
  but differs in emphasis: GTD organises all commitments; Lean is
  specifically about de-risking uncertain ones before over-investing.
