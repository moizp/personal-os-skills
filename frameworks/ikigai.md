# Framework: Ikigai

Generic summary for use by Skills.

## Core ideas relevant to planning

- **Four circles, one overlap.** Ikigai ("a reason for being") is
  traditionally drawn as four overlapping circles: what you love, what
  you're good at, what the world needs, and what you can be paid for. The
  small overlap at the centre of all four is your ikigai — not any one
  circle alone.

- **A goal in only one or two circles is a warning sign, not neutral.**
  - Love + good at, but no world-needs/paid-for grounding → a hobby, not
    a long-term commitment (fine if that's the honest intent, not fine if
    it's meant to carry real weight).
  - Good at + paid for, but no love → a job that will eventually produce
    burnout, however competent the output.
  - Love + world needs, but no paid-for → meaningful but financially
    unsustainable as a primary goal; may be fine as a secondary one.
  - Good at + world needs, but no love → comfortable and useful, but
    unlikely to sustain motivation over a genuinely long horizon.

- **This is a long-term-direction tool, not a task-planning tool.**
  Ikigai answers "why," at the scale of years — it doesn't produce
  quarterly milestones or daily todos directly. Those come from applying
  Seven Habits' roles and Big Rocks *underneath* an ikigai-informed
  long-term goal.

- **Gaps are informative, not failures.** Not every role or area of life
  needs a fully-overlapping ikigai match right now. A role with a clear
  gap (e.g. "I'm good at this and it's needed, but I don't love it") is
  useful information for deciding what to change, not something to paper
  over with a forced narrative.

## How this should show up in the system

- Long-term goal-setting should work through the four circles explicitly,
  per relevant role, rather than jumping straight to "what do I want."
- A long-term goal should be traceable to where it sits in that role's
  ikigai — and if it only sits in one or two circles, that's worth
  naming, not smoothing over.
- Don't force a long-term goal into existence for every role just for
  symmetry — an honest gap is more useful than a manufactured direction.
- Yearly and quarterly goals (see quarterly-goal-setting) should ultimately
  trace back to a long-term goal grounded this way — this is the layer
  above "which role does this serve," not a replacement for it.
